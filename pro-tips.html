Brushland Pro Tips

Advanced painting technique for turning loose strokes into a kingdom that looks designed.

🖌️ Brush Control

Three-Pass Approach

Advanced painters never finish a region in one stroke. Build the map in three passes so each layer supports the next.

  • Base: plains and meadow fill the whole area first
  • Middle: hills, rivers and lakes carve the structure in
  • Top: mountains, forests and detail sit on the finished land

Scale Matching

Your brush size should match the detail level you want. A single large stroke that is twice the width of the target region reads as a deliberate bold shape. A small stroke across a huge area reads as a mistake.

  • Small brush for coastlines and river ends
  • Large brush for mountain ranges and open plains
  • Never let a brush extend past the map edge unevenly

Fade, Do Not Cut

Hard edges are the giveaway of an amateur map. Taper your strokes so terrain blends into its neighbour instead of stopping abruptly.

  • Reduce pressure at the end of every stroke
  • Overlap edges by a small amount to blend
  • Leave a band of transition between biome types

Undo as a Design Tool

The undo brush is not only for mistakes. It lets you carve natural-looking shorelines by removing a little material and letting the edge settle.

  • Use it to soften a blunt lake edge into a bay
  • Use it to carve a narrow land bridge between two banks
  • Use it to erase an accidental structure before it spawns

📐 Terrain Ratios That Actually Work

Recommended Land Mix

Terrain Share of Map What It Produces Why That Amount
Plains 30 to 35% Farms, manors, markets The backbone. Too little and the kingdom has no food base.
Hills 20 to 25% Orchards, windmills, churches The most versatile terrain, so it deserves the second largest share.
Forest 20 to 25% Sawmills, hunting lodges Keep it varied in density. Solid blocks look flat.
Water 15 to 20% Mills, fisheries, bridges Rivers carry the composition, so keep them connected.
Mountains 10 to 15% Quarries, watchtowers Best used as a frame around the edge rather than a centrepiece.

The 1:1 Rule

For every water feature you draw, plan one adjacent meadow or plain. Water without nearby flat land produces fisheries but no housing, and the settlement looks abandoned.

The River Spine

One long river crossing the map gives you mills, bridges and a natural transport line. Then add two or three short tributaries rather than several separate rivers.

The Frame Rule

Put mountains or dense forest along two edges of the map. A framed kingdom looks finished, while an open-edged one looks like a work in progress.

🖼️ Composition and Visual Flow

Reading a Map Like a Painter

The most satisfying Brushland kingdoms have a clear visual hierarchy. When you look at the map, your eye should travel through it in a deliberate order rather than landing randomly on whichever cluster is densest.

  • • Establish a focal point. A village hall or a fountain at the centre gives the eye somewhere to land first.
  • • Create a path. A clear route from the outer cottages toward the centre makes the kingdom feel like a place people live.
  • • Break the symmetry. Perfectly mirrored layouts read as artificial. Offset one district slightly.
  • • Use elevation as hierarchy. Put humble cottages low and landmark buildings high.
  • • Leave breathing room. Empty meadow between districts is what makes the dense areas look intentional.

Transition Zones

Where two terrain types meet, leave a narrow band that belongs to neither. Real landscapes have hedges, beaches and tree lines at their borders, and this one detail does more for believability than any single building.

Varied Cluster Size

Repeat a motif but vary its scale. A village of five cottages, then a village of two, then a market square of one, creates rhythm. Ten identical clusters create wallpaper.

Troubleshooting Advanced Problems

Buildings still not spawning after a big repaint

Some structures need the terrain around them, not just under them. A fishery wants a wide water body. A sawmill wants forest with depth. Repaint a small test patch with a larger brush, wait for growth to catch up, and check the requirements in the building guide.

Water does not flow where you expect

Water follows the land you drew. If a river stops in the middle of nowhere, the ground beneath it is not descending. Lengthen the slope with a couple of gentle strokes before extending the channel.

Pro Tips

  • ★ Work in greyscale first: lay down all the land shapes, then add the terrain colours
  • ★ Draw the river network first, then build the land it flows through around it
  • ★ Zoom out regularly to check the composition, then zoom back in for detail
  • ★ Cluster decorations in threes or fives, since odd groups look more natural than even ones
  • ★ Keep a second map as a blank canvas for experiments before committing to a full design
  • ★ Try the same terrain recipe at different scales, since a small kingdom and a large one need different ratios
