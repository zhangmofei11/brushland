Kingdom Tips

Expert advice for creating beautiful, thriving kingdoms in Brushland.

🌟 Getting Started

Begin with a Vision

Before drawing, imagine what kind of kingdom you want. A mountainous retreat? A riverside trading hub? A pastoral farming village? Having a vision guides your terrain choices.

Start Small

Begin with a modest map and expand as you learn. A small, well-designed kingdom teaches you terrain-building relationships before you tackle large-scale projects.

Follow Natural Patterns

Study real landscapes. Rivers flow downhill, forests grow on hillsides, farms occupy flat land. Mirroring real geography makes your kingdom feel authentic.

Patience Rewards Creativity

There's no rush. Let buildings grow organically. If a building doesn't appear where you expected, adjust your terrain rather than forcing it.

🎯 Design Principles

Create Visual Flow

Great kingdoms tell a story as you move through them. Guide the viewer's eye with:

  • • A river that winds through the entire map, connecting different regions
  • • Mountains that frame one side, creating a natural boundary
  • • A clear path from the humble village outskirts to the grand central square

Balance variety with unity

A monotonous kingdom is boring, but chaos is overwhelming. Aim for:

  • • 2-3 distinct terrain zones (forest region, plains region, lakeside)
  • • Repeating motifs (similar building clusters) to create rhythm
  • • Transition areas where terrain types blend naturally

Use Negative Space

Empty space isn't wasted — it's breathing room. Not every inch needs buildings. Leave some areas as wild meadows, empty plains, or quiet forests to give the eye places to rest.

✨ Advanced Techniques

Terrain Layering

Draw terrain in passes: base layer (plains), middle layer (hills, water), top layer (mountains, forests). Each pass affects what buildings appear in areas below.

Water Management

Create natural river systems by drawing a main river with smaller tributaries. Wide rivers support more fisheries; narrow streams are charming but limited.

Elevation Storytelling

Place simple cottages low and grand buildings high. This creates a visual hierarchy and tells the story of a kingdom growing up from humble beginnings.

Forest Density

Dense, large forests attract more wildlife buildings. Sparse, thin forests create better lumber areas. Vary density to create different forest character.

District Planning

Group similar buildings intentionally. An industrial district near water, a residential quarter in the plains, a religious area on hills — create functional zones.

Seasonal Themes

Create maps that evoke seasons: spring with many rivers, summer with vast plains, autumn with colorful forests, winter with snowy mountains.

Troubleshooting

Buildings aren't appearing

Make sure your terrain is in the right conditions. Check the building guide for requirements. Also ensure you have enough of the supporting terrain type.

Kingdom looks chaotic

Try to create distinct zones for different building types. Too much variety in a small area creates visual noise. Group similar buildings together.

Want more of one building type

Expand the supporting terrain. More forest = more sawmills and hunting lodges. More river = more mills and fisheries. The ratio matters.

Pro Tips

  • ★ Save different terrain "recipes" as you discover combinations you like
  • ★ Use the undo brush to create natural-looking shoreline erosion
  • ★ Add small isolated terrain features for visual interest (a lone tree, tiny pond)
  • ★ Border your kingdom with mountains or dense forest for a framed look
  • ★ The time of day affects how buildings are highlighted — experiment with lighting
  • ★ Take screenshots of your favorite moments — these kingdoms are worth preserving
